<p>Block Out! looks simple — push colored blocks through matching gates — but the levels get hard fast. By level 30 you&#39;ll find yourself staring at a board for two minutes before making a single move. By level 50 you&#39;ll be retrying levels six or seven times.</p>
<p>This guide collects the tactics that actually move the needle for new players. None of these are obscure tricks; they&#39;re the habits that separate someone who solves a level in three tries from someone who solves it on the first attempt.</p>
<h2>1. Read the board before you move anything</h2>
<p>This is the single most important habit. Most failures in Block Out! happen because a player slid the first piece they noticed instead of the first piece they should have moved.</p>
<p>Before your first move, identify:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Every gate&#39;s color</strong> — note the position and color of each exit</li>
<li><strong>Which blocks are pinned</strong> — blocks that can&#39;t move until something else moves first</li>
<li><strong>Choke points</strong> — the narrowest part of the board, usually near the center</li>
</ul>
<p>If two pieces both want the same lane, decide who goes first <strong>before</strong> you start sliding. Reversing a wrong choice usually costs you 4–5 moves you didn&#39;t need to spend.</p>
<h2>2. Solve gates in dependency order, not visual order</h2>
<p>Beginners tend to solve from top to bottom or left to right. That&#39;s almost never right.</p>
<p>The correct order is <strong>dependency order</strong>: which block, if removed first, would unlock the most other blocks? That&#39;s your starting move.</p>
<p>For example, if a long blue piece is wedged across the middle of the board, it&#39;s probably blocking 3–4 other pieces. Get the blue piece out first — even if it seems harder — because everything else gets easier afterward.</p>
<h2>3. Use &quot;parking moves&quot; — don&#39;t be afraid to move backward</h2>
<p>A parking move is when you slide a piece <strong>away</strong> from its gate just to clear a path for another piece. New players resist this because it feels like wasted effort.</p>
<p>It isn&#39;t. Parking is one of the core mechanics of color-sort puzzles. The optimal solution to many Block Out! levels involves moving a piece two or three squares in the wrong direction first, then routing it back later.</p>
<p>If you&#39;re stuck and every piece seems to want the same lane, look for a piece you can park temporarily. The board will open up.</p>
<h2>4. Plan three moves ahead, not just one</h2>
<p>Easy levels (1–10) are forgiving — you can play move-by-move and still solve them. Past level 15, that approach falls apart.</p>
<p>The shift to make: before you slide a block, ask yourself &quot;what does the board look like <strong>after</strong> this move, and what does my next move look like?&quot; If you can&#39;t see two clean moves ahead, the move you&#39;re about to make is probably wrong.</p>
<p>This sounds slow. It&#39;s actually faster, because every wrong move costs you an undo and a retry.</p>
<h2>5. Use undo, not restart, when you&#39;re learning a level</h2>
<p>Block Out! gives you an undo button. Use it.</p>
<p>If you make a move and immediately realize it was wrong, undo it — don&#39;t restart the level. Restarting wipes your mental map of the board. Undoing keeps your progress and lets you try the alternative.</p>
<p>Save restarts for when you&#39;re 4+ moves deep into a clearly broken plan. Then it&#39;s worth starting fresh.</p>
<h2>6. Watch the video walkthrough at 0.5x speed for hard levels</h2>
<p>For Hard, Expert, or Super Hard levels, our <a href="/en/level/">video walkthroughs</a> cover every level. But there&#39;s a trick to using them well: <strong>don&#39;t watch at full speed</strong>.</p>
<p>Drop to 0.5x speed and watch the <strong>first 5 moves only</strong>. Those moves usually define the entire solution. Once you understand the opening, pause the video and try to finish on your own. You&#39;ll learn faster than if you copy every move.</p>
<h2>7. Recognize the three failure patterns</h2>
<p>Most stuck levels fall into one of three patterns:</p>
<p><strong>Pattern A: &quot;Box-in&quot;</strong> — You moved a block that&#39;s now trapped between two others, with no way out. Fix: undo immediately. If you can&#39;t, restart.</p>
<p><strong>Pattern B: &quot;Gate collision&quot;</strong> — Two pieces want the same gate and you committed to the wrong one. Fix: there&#39;s almost always a parking move that fixes this. Look for an empty cell that can hold the wrong-order piece.</p>
<p><strong>Pattern C: &quot;Long-piece runway&quot;</strong> — A long horizontal or vertical piece needs a clear lane and you filled the lane with smaller pieces. Fix: undo back to before you blocked the lane. Long pieces should usually move first.</p>
<p>Recognizing which pattern you&#39;re in saves you minutes per level.</p>
<h2>8. Don&#39;t burn boosters on Easy levels</h2>
<p>Block Out! gives you free boosters and lets you earn more by playing. New players often spend them on Easy levels (1–20) because the boosters are flashy.</p>
<p>Save them. By level 50 you&#39;ll genuinely need every booster you have, and earning more gets harder. Treat coins and boosters like an emergency fund.</p>
<h2>9. Take breaks on Super Hard levels</h2>
<p>Super Hard levels are designed to be retry-heavy. If you&#39;ve failed the same level 5+ times in a row, your brain has locked into a wrong mental model and you&#39;re not going to solve it by trying again.</p>
<p>Close the app. Come back in 30 minutes. The solution often becomes obvious on the first attempt after a break — your fresh eyes spot the dependency you missed.</p>
<h2>10. Learn the &quot;lock piece&quot; concept</h2>
<p>Every hard level has a <strong>lock piece</strong> — the one block that, until it moves, traps everything else. Identifying the lock piece is the single biggest unlock (pun intended) you can make.</p>
<p>The lock piece is usually:</p>
<ul>
<li>A long piece spanning the middle</li>
<li>A piece in the densest corner</li>
<li>A piece whose only path crosses every other gate</li>
</ul>
<p>Once you&#39;ve identified the lock piece, your entire plan becomes &quot;how do I move the lock piece, and what setup do I need to do that?&quot; Everything else slots into place around that.</p>
<h2>Putting it all together</h2>
<p>You don&#39;t have to apply all ten tips at once. Pick two or three for your next session — say, <strong>read the board first</strong> and <strong>use undo, not restart</strong> — and let them become habit. The other tips will fall into place naturally as the levels get harder.</p>
<p>Block Out! rewards patience. The players who finish level 200 aren&#39;t faster than the players who quit at level 50; they&#39;re just better at slowing down before the first move.</p>
<p>For level-specific solutions, our <a href="/en/level/">video walkthrough index</a> covers every level from 1 to 200.</p>
<h2>Quick reference</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Read first, move second.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Solve in dependency order.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Park pieces — backward moves are fine.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Plan three moves ahead.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Undo &gt; restart for single mistakes.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Drop video to 0.5x for hard levels.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Save boosters for level 50+.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Take a break after 5 failures.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Find the lock piece.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Long pieces need clear runways.</strong></li>
</ul>
<p>Good luck out there.</p>
